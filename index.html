Web Security Check

A quick check for websites, web apps and phone apps. Plain words. About 15 minutes.

How to use this check

  • Read each question. Pick Yes, No or Not sure.
  • If you are not sure, that counts as a No. Ask your web developer or host.
  • Use Part 1 for websites. Use Part 2 for web apps. Use Part 3 for phone apps.
  • Many sites are also web apps. If your site has logins, do Part 1 and Part 2.
  • Each part gets its own score, and you get a fix list built from your answers.
  • Your answers stay on this page. Nothing is sent anywhere unless you choose to WhatsApp us your results.

Stay safe and fair

  • Only check sites and apps that you own or have written permission to check.
  • Never try to get into someone else's site or app.
  • Do any testing on a test copy if you can, not on live customer data.

Part 1: Your website

For any business website.

1
Does your site show the padlock?The address starts with https. The browser shows no warnings.
2
Is everything up to date?Your site system (like WordPress), themes and plugins. Old ones are a common way in. Delete ones you do not use.
3
Do you use strong, different passwords?Long and not used anywhere else. A password manager helps.
4
Is two-step login turned on?Turn it on for your site admin, your hosting and your domain. It asks for a code as well as a password.
5
Do only the right people have admin access?Remove old staff, old developers and anyone who left. Give each person their own login.
6
Have you changed the default admin name?Names like "admin" are easy to guess. Pick something else.
7
Do you have backups, and have you tried one?Backups should run by themselves and be kept away from the site. A backup you never tried may not work.
8
Are your forms protected from spam?Contact forms should have spam protection. They should only take the details you need.
9
Are old copies and test pages gone?Old test sites, spare copies and backup files left online can leak private things.
10
Does your domain renew on its own?If a domain runs out, someone else can take it. Switch on auto-renew. Keep the contact email current.

Part 2: Your web app

A web app is a site where people log in and do things, like book, pay or see their account.

1
Can people only see their own data?Make two test accounts, A and B. Log in as A. Open a record. Change the number in the address bar to one of B's. The app should say no.
2
Are admin pages blocked for normal users?Log in as a normal user. Try to open an admin page. It should be blocked. Mark Yes if it is blocked.
3
Does the app slow down guessing?After many wrong passwords, it should lock the account for a while or slow things down.
4
Are passwords stored safely?Ask your developer. Passwords must never be saved as plain text. They should be "hashed", which means scrambled so no one can read them.
5
Is two-step login offered?At least for admins. Better for everyone.
6
Does logout work, and do old logins expire?After you log out, the back button should not open your account. Idle sessions should end after a while.
7
Does the app check what people type in?Ask your developer. The server must check forms and search boxes. Error messages should be short and must not show code or database details.
8
Are secret keys kept out of the code?Ask your developer. Passwords, API keys and database logins must not sit in the code or in public places.
9
Are the tools the app uses kept up to date?Apps are built from many parts made by others. Old parts can have known holes. Someone should check them often.
10
Does the app keep a record of odd activity?Failed logins and strange actions should be written down, and someone should look at them. Only keep the personal information you need (POPIA).

Part 3: Your phone app

For apps people install on a phone.

1
Does the app only use secure connections?All data between the app and your server must travel over https.
2
Does the server check every request?Hiding a button in the app is not security. The server must check who the user is every time. Do the two-account test from Part 2.
3
Are secret keys kept out of the app?Anyone can open an app file and look inside. Do not put passwords or private keys in it.
4
Is login information stored safely on the phone?Ask your developer. It should use the phone's safe storage, not plain files.
5
Does the app ask only for what it needs?Check its permissions. Does it need the camera, contacts or location? If not, it should not ask.
6
Can you stop old app versions?If a version has a bad hole, you should be able to make people update.
7
Is a lost or stolen phone covered?The app should have a lock (PIN or fingerprint). You should be able to end a user's login from the server.
8
Are the app's tools kept up to date?Like Part 2, number 9. Old parts can have known holes.

What this check can and can't do

This check finds common, easy problems. It can't find every weak spot. A proper security test looks deeper at your code, your logins and how your app handles data. Then it tells you what to fix first.
